# The Womb: research for the break-details page

Researched 2026-10-10. Spot: The Womb, south of Gracetown, Margaret River region, WA (approx 114.982 E, 33.895 S).
Every source below was opened and read; no fact comes from memory. Quotes are under 15 words.

## Read this first: how independent the sources are

Most online surf guides for The Womb copy one original write-up. The source groups are:

- **Group W (WannaSurf and copies).** WannaSurf's spot page is the original. surfspots.co copies it word for word. Mondo paraphrases it closely ("400 meters north of Ellenbrook Bombie", "more than one pro surfer"). SurfSphere's field list (S/SW swell, E/NE wind, mid/high tide, intermediate) matches it. The margaretriver.com Womb page rewords the same paragraph ("pitched straight into a short, intense barrel"). freolongblack.com pastes WannaSurf's access text.
- **Group F (Surf-Forecast).** Surf-Forecast's own text. freolongblack.com also pastes this text word for word.
- **Independent of both:** margaretriver.com locals' guide, Wikipedia, DBCA Explore Parks, ExplorOz, Swellnet, Landgate / WA Government, inHerit (WA heritage), National Trust WA, surfingdownsouth.com.au, a Cape to Cape walking blog.

So "2+ sources" for a surf fact often means 2+ publishers, not 2+ independent observations. Where a fact rests only on Group W, it says **(Group W only)**. Under the rules it still counts, but it is weaker.

### Settled values (the cautious versions)

- **Swell: SW, 225°. Best range SSW to WSW, 202.5°–247.5°.** SW comes from 4 publishers (Group W). Surf-Forecast (Group F) says WSW (247.5°).
  - **Contested:** Group W also lists South (180°), and Mondo's sector reaches 157.5° (SSE). Surf-Forecast says nothing about south. The range that both groups support runs from SW to WSW. 180° is supported by Group W only.
- **Wind: E (90°). Best range NE to E, 45°–90°.** E comes from 5 publishers across both groups. NE (45°) comes from Group W only. Mondo's wider sector (22.5°–112.5°) is a bucket width, not an observation.
- **Tide: mid and high.** 4 publishers, all Group W, which is effectively one origin. Surf-Forecast gives no tide. No source says low tide works. Cautious version: mid to high, and treat low tide as not recommended (unconfirmed).
- **Size: 3–8+ ft. Best barrels at head-high and above.** WannaSurf and surfspots.co give 3–5 ft to start and 8 ft+ at the top. WannaSurf and margaretriver.com both say it turns on above head height. No source gives an upper limit beyond "8ft+".
- **Season:** **Single-source (do not use).** Surf-Forecast's model says February / summer is best for clean waves ("clean 50%").

## story

### Where the name "The Womb" comes from

- **Single-source (do not use).** A user review on Surf-Forecast says a boyfriend named it in 1986 from a picture of a foetus in a barrel. Their words: "named The Womb from a picture of a fetus in a barrel". This is one unverified user comment. No guide, book or news source confirms it.

### Noongar names (openly published sources only)

- **Mokidup.** The Noongar name for the Ellensbrook homestead site, a traditional camping place.
  - Sources: Wikipedia, Ellensbrook ("a Noongar camping ground named Mokidup"); inHerit, WA Government heritage register ("The Noongar know the place as Mokidup"); National Trust WA, whose official site name is "Ellensbrook at Mokidup on Wadandi Noongar Country".
  - Licences: Wikipedia is CC BY-SA. inHerit is "copyright Government of Western Australia". National Trust is standard copyright. None of the three pages carries a non-commercial licence or an ICIP / "ask the Custodians" notice.
  - Use only the place name. inHerit also has a Dreaming story about the nearby cave; it was deliberately not copied here.
- **Meekadarribee (Falls / Cave).** A named site near the homestead.
  - Sources: Wikipedia, Ellensbrook ("Meekadarribee Cave"); Wikipedia, Yebble ("Meekadarribee Falls"); Landgate ("Meekadarribee Falls").
  - Wikipedia and Landgate call it significant to Wardandi people. Leave the meaning out of the game: only one source (inHerit) gives it.
- **Yebble.** The official locality name since 2021, covering Ellensbrook and mostly national park.
  - Sources: Landgate; WA Government media statement; Wikipedia, Yebble.
  - It was Samuel Isaacs' Aboriginal name. Landgate: "Yebble, Mr Isaacs Aboriginal name". It was "excised from... Gracetown and Burnside".
  - Not confirmed whether The Womb's exact spot falls inside Yebble or inside Gracetown. Say "near Yebble".
- **Traditional Owners.** DBCA: "We recognise and acknowledge Wardandi people as the Traditional Owners". National Trust spells it "Wadandi"; Wikipedia's Yebble article spells it "Wardandi". Both spellings are in use.

### Nearby history (2+ sources)

- **Gracetown is named after Grace Bussell.**
  - Sources: Wikipedia, Gracetown ("local Western Australian heroine Grace Bussell"); Wikipedia, Grace Bussell; surfingdownsouth.com.au ("named in honour of Grace Bussell").
  - Note: Wikipedia, Gracetown and Wikipedia, Grace Bussell come from the same publisher. surfingdownsouth is the independent second source.
- **Gracetown surveyed 1961; lots sold and town gazetted 1963.**
  - Sources: Wikipedia, Gracetown; surfingdownsouth.com.au ("not surveyed until 1961"; "sale of lots began in 1963").
- **1876 SS Georgette rescue: Grace Bussell and Sam Isaacs (Yebble) rescued passengers.**
  - Sources: Landgate ("rescued 54 passengers from the shipwrecked SS Georgette"); WA Government media statement; Wikipedia, Grace Bussell; Wikipedia, Yebble.
  - Isaacs later received 100 acres of land, the first land grant to an Aboriginal person in WA. Sources: WA Government statement; Landgate; Wikipedia, Yebble says he was granted land.
- **Ellensbrook homestead, built for Alfred and Ellen Bussell.**
  - Sources: Wikipedia, Ellensbrook; inHerit; National Trust WA ("Explore the 1857 homestead").
  - **Contested date:** Wikipedia and the National Trust say 1857. inHerit says "Construction began in 1855". Cautious version: "built from the mid-1850s".
  - Wikipedia: built partly with local Noongar labour, and named after Ellen Bussell.
- **Ellensbrook homestead is now run by the National Trust of WA.**
  - Sources: Wikipedia, Ellensbrook; National Trust WA booking page; ExplorOz.
- **1996 Gracetown cliff collapse.**
  - Sources: Wikipedia, Gracetown ("five adults and four children were killed in a cliff collapse"); the Cape to Cape walk blog (passes the "Gracetown Tragedy Memorial").
  - Death toll and details: **single-source (do not use)**. The blog only names the memorial. This is a sensitive event; don't use it in the game without Andrew's call.
- **Ellensbrook Bombie, the big-wave spot next to The Womb.**
  - Sources: margaretriver.com locals' guide (Ellensbrook Bombie produces "waves in the 15-20 foot range"); WannaSurf ("The bombie gets almost as big a Margarets Main break").

## Couldn't confirm

- **Origin of the name "The Womb":** only one user review (Surf-Forecast).
- **Reef rock type:** sources say "coral / sharp rocks" or just "reef". No source says limestone.
- **"Shallow reef":** no source uses "shallow" about The Womb itself. margaretriver.com uses it for The Box.
- **Whether the opposite-breaking wave is a rideable right:** sources only describe it as closing the left's barrel.
- **4WD:** DBCA says "Accessible by road"; no source states 2WD/4WD, gravel or sealed.
- **Walk time:** WannaSurf says 5–15 min; DBCA and the locals' guide say "long" / "lengthy". Exact distance from the car park is unconfirmed (about 1 km by coordinates, estimated, not sourced).
- **Exact distance to Lefthanders:** 500 m vs 0.56 mi.
- **Rips and isolation as Womb-specific hazards:** Surf-Forecast only.
- **Low-tide behaviour:** no source covers it.
- **Size ceiling:** nothing beyond "8ft+".
- **Best season:** Surf-Forecast model only.
- **Skill rating on Surf-Forecast:** the "Difficulty 1.0" box is from 1 vote and sits under another break's label, so it is unreliable.
- **Whether The Womb lies inside the Yebble locality boundary or inside Gracetown.**
- **No Noongar place name for The Womb's own stretch of reef/coast** was found in an open source. Only nearby names were found (Mokidup, Meekadarribee, Yebble).
