## What surprised me

1. **The slope catch doesn't solve the take-off.** At the spot the face goes from slope 0.2 to 0.7 (2.0 at the top) in
   about 0.4 s, then is gone. Moving 9 m/s, it is 3–4 m long. The rider reaches 3.7 m/s (assist 4), 5.2 (8) or 5.5 (12)
   when caught; nothing in the physics carries her from there to near c before the face has passed. The floating board's
   heave (0.5 s critically damped) lifts her straight onto the crest. A real slab late-drop works because the wave stands
   up and pitches *at* the surfer, and the water near the crest moves forward at nearly c, and because the face is
   several metres long. The model gives her neither the face length nor the forward-moving water at the crest. The
   reviewer suspected the bot (it paddled 8–9 m in from the first frame, onto the onset). With it waiting and paddling
   only for the last 2 s it now pops up at 6 ft but rides 1.1–2.8 s at every Experience level, so the finding holds.
2. The breaking criterion's real H/depth at onset is ~1.0, not γ/(1+γδ) = 0.67, because `breakingDepth`'s deep-water
   exponent (above 7 m) and the shoreward-only travel smoothing (σ 8 m on a 15 m face) both lag the onset up the face.
3. The rim's leftover isn't a seat constant. It's `sectionPoint`'s w-weighted sheet-column Hermite between the trough
   knot and the front knot: that knot span's tangent comes from the floor knot, whose sheet value is up the face.
4. Four fixture invariants that held at 4 ft fail at 6 ft. One that matters: on the first leg's middle, a marginal 6 ft
   break's time since onset grows about 6× too slowly (the record interpolating between a level that broke and one that
   didn't), so its collapse never starts. That is "one curl, one clock" (R2) showing up in R1.
